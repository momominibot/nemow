🏃 Activity Guide by Cycle Phase

Match your movement to your hormones — work with your body, not against it.
🩸 Menstrual (Days 1–5)
Gentle · Low intensity
🧘
Restorative yoga, walking, stretching, light swimming. Gentle movement increases blood flow and reduces pain.
🚫
Avoid: high-intensity training, inversion yoga poses, heavy lifting.
Why: Your body is losing blood, energy is lower, and prostaglandins are causing cramping. Gentle movement improves pelvic circulation and releases endorphins. Exercise reduces dysmenorrhea pain i; yoga specifically reduces menstrual pain i.
🌱 Follicular (Days 6–13)
High intensity · Power phase
🔥
HIIT, strength training, running, cycling, dance. This is your body's strongest window for intense training.
💪
Focus on: progressive overload, heavy lifts, longer endurance sessions.
Why: Estrogen is rising → improved insulin sensitivity, higher energy, better training adaptation, and enhanced muscle protein synthesis. Your body handles intense activity well during this phase i.
✨ Ovulation (Day 14)
Moderate · Balanced
🚶
Brisk walking, yoga, Pilates, social activities. Enjoy your peak energy without overtaxing your body.
👯
Great for: group classes, outdoor activities, and social workouts.
Why: Energy is at its peak, but your body is in the vulnerable ovulation window. Moderate activity is safest — significant metabolic stress can suppress ovulation. Ovulation itself is a sign of metabolic health i.
🌙 Luteal (Days 15–28)
Moderate → gentle · Taper down
🧘‍♀️
Pilates, yoga, swimming, moderate strength, walking. Maintain consistency but reduce intensity.
📉
Reduce intensity as your period approaches (last 3–5 days). Listen to your body.
Why: Progesterone is rising → body temperature is higher, fatigue may increase, and PMS symptoms can appear. Exercise helps with mood and cravings, but don't overdo it. Complex carbs and magnesium support this phase i.
