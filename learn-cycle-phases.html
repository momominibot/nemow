The Four Phases of Your Cycle
verifiedReviewed by Dr. Priya Menon, OB-GYN · 4 min read
🩸 Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)

The uterine lining sheds as estrogen and progesterone hit their lowest point. This is your body's reset. You may feel lower energy — rest is productive here. Menstrual blood colour and texture provide clues about hormone balance.

🌱 Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)

FSH recruits follicles, and the dominant follicle begins producing estrogen. Energy rises, mood lifts, and cervical mucus transitions from dry to creamy to egg-white. This phase varies most in length between women.

🥚 Ovulation (Day 14 ± 3)

A surge in LH triggers egg release. Estrogen peaks then dips; progesterone begins rising. You may feel a twinge (mittelschmerz), see egg-white mucus, and notice a temperature shift the next morning. The fertile window spans ~6 days.

🌙 Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)

Progesterone dominates, thickening the endometrium and raising your basal body temperature. PMS symptoms may emerge from breast tenderness to mood shifts. Without implantation, progesterone falls and menstruation begins anew.

Cycle length varies. A "normal" cycle ranges from 21–35 days. The follicular phase is the variable part — the luteal phase is typically 12–14 days. Track at least 3 cycles to understand your personal pattern.
