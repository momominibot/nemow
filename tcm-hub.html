Cramps Relief Acupoints

Five evidence-based acupressure points to ease menstrual pain, warm the uterus, and move Qi and blood.

1
SP6 Sanyinjiao 三阴交 Primary
Location 3 cun (4 finger-widths) above the inner ankle bone, just behind the shin bone (tibia).
back_hand Massage Thumb press, firm until a clear aching sensation (de qi). 6s pressure + 2s rest. 5 min per leg, 2 cycles both legs = 20 min total.
schedule When During pain (first 2 days for spasmodic cramps; 2 days before expected period for congestive type).
TCM: Convergence point of the Spleen, Liver, and Kidney yin meridians — the most important gynecological acupoint. Moves blood, resolves stasis, regulates menstruation, and calms the uterus.
Multiple RCTs confirm statistically significant pain reduction with SP6 acupressure i i.
2
CV4 Guanyuan 关元
Location 3 cun below the navel on the midline of the abdomen.
back_hand Massage Circular press with the palm or thumb, gentle to moderate pressure. 1-2 minutes.
schedule When Before and during menstruation. Can be combined with a warm compress for enhanced effect.
TCM: "Gate of Origin" — tonifies Yuan (source) Qi, warms the uterus, nourishes blood, and strengthens the lower Jiao. One of the most important points for gynecological health and reproductive wellness.
️ Caution: Avoid strong abdominal massage if experiencing heavy bleeding. Use gentle circular pressure only. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
3
LV3 Taichong 太冲
Location Dorsum of the foot, in the depression between the 1st and 2nd metatarsal bones.
back_hand Massage Thumb press, moderate to firm pressure. 1 minute per foot.
schedule When During cramps, especially when accompanied by emotional stress, irritability, or breast tenderness.
TCM: Source point of the Liver meridian. Spreads Liver Qi, resolves stagnation, clears Liver fire, and relieves pain. Essential for cramps caused by Liver Qi stagnation (the most common pattern in primary dysmenorrhea).
4
SP8 Diji 地机
Location 3 cun below the medial knee, on the posterior border of the tibia (shin bone).
back_hand Massage Thumb press, firm pressure. 1-2 minutes per leg.
schedule When During acute cramps — this point is especially effective for sudden, severe pain.
TCM: Xi-cleft (accumulation) point of the Spleen meridian. Xi-cleft points are specifically used for acute pain and acute conditions. Regulates the Spleen, moves blood, and resolves stasis — excellent for acute dysmenorrhea flare-ups.
5
ST36 Zusanli 足三里
Location 3 cun below the knee, 1 finger-width lateral to the anterior crest of the tibia.
back_hand Massage Thumb press, firm pressure until aching. 1-2 minutes per leg.
schedule When Daily for long-term benefit, and during cramps for support. Especially helpful if cramps are accompanied by fatigue, bloating, or loose stools.
TCM: One of the most important tonifying points in the body. Strengthens Spleen and Stomach, boosts Qi and blood production, supports immunity, and promotes general wellness. A strong Spleen ensures adequate blood production and smooth menstruation.
5-Minute Quick Routine For acute relief

Press each point for ~1 minute per side, moving through the sequence. Repeat both sides where applicable.

1️⃣ SP6 → 2️⃣ CV4 → 3️⃣ LV3 → 4️⃣ SP8 → 5️⃣ ST36

📅 When to Massage

🌱
Preventive: Start 2-3 days BEFORE your expected period. Daily acupressure in the premenstrual phase can reduce pain intensity when bleeding begins.
bloodtype
Acute relief: During the first 2-3 days of menses, when cramps are typically strongest. Use the full 20-minute routine or the 5-minute quick routine as needed.
⏱️
Duration: 20-minute full routine (SP6 5min/leg × 2 cycles + other points), or 5-minute quick routine. Consistency over multiple cycles yields the best results.
️
Caution: Avoid strong abdominal massage (CV4) if experiencing heavy bleeding. All abdominal points are contraindicated in pregnancy. Use warm hands for comfort.
🔥
Enhance: Use warm hands, and apply a warm compress or heating pad to the lower abdomen after acupressure for synergistic pain relief. Warmth improves local blood flow and relaxes uterine muscles.
