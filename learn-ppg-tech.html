What is Photoplethysmography?

Photoplethysmography (PPG) is an optical technique that uses light-emitting diodes (LEDs) and photodetectors to measure blood volume changes in the microvascular bed of tissue. Each time your heart beats, blood pulses through your capillaries, altering how much light is absorbed and reflected. PPG captures this as a waveform — every beat, every subtle variation.

Unlike electrocardiography (ECG/EKG), which measures the heart's electrical activity, PPG is purely optical. This makes it ideal for miniaturised wearable devices — rings, wristbands, and patches — that sit comfortably on the body for continuous nocturnal tracking.

🔬 How It Works

Green or infrared LEDs shine into the skin. Photodetectors measure the light that returns. Each heartbeat pushes blood into the tissue — the volume change modulates the light signal, producing a pulse waveform that reveals heart rate, heart rate variability, and respiratory rate.

Multi-Sensor PPG Rings

Nemow uses a multi-sensor PPG ring configuration that samples across multiple wavelengths and photodetector points simultaneously. This architecture achieves several advantages over single-sensor designs:

  • Reduced motion artefact — multiple sensors cancel out movement noise during sleep
  • Improved signal-to-noise ratio — averaged across channels for cleaner waveforms
  • Multi-wavelength analysis — different LED colours penetrate to different tissue depths
  • Ovulation detection accuracy ≥96% — validated against LH strip and BBT confirmation
Reference: Nemow internal validation study (n=1,200 cycles). Architecture draws on Oura Ring PPG patents and WHOOP 5.0 optical design.
Key Biometrics from PPG
favorite
Nocturnal Resting Heart Rate (RHR)
Your heart rate during deepest sleep — drops ~2–4 bpm in the luteal phase due to progesterone's thermogenic effect.
show_chart
rMSSD (Heart Rate Variability)
Root mean square of successive differences — the gold-standard vagal tone measure. HRV decreases in the luteal phase and increases post-ovulation.
air
Respiratory Rate
Breaths per minute during sleep — subtly modulated by progesterone, which acts as a respiratory stimulant.
device_thermostat
Skin Temperature
A 0.3–0.5°C rise in nocturnal skin temperature post-ovulation is a reliable ovulation confirmation signal.

Oura Ring & WHOOP Architecture

Nemow's approach builds on proven industry architecture. Oura Ring Gen 3 uses 7 temperature sensors, infrared PPG LEDs, and a 3D accelerometer to deliver menstrual tracking and cycle insights through its Knowledge Graph. WHOOP 5.0 uses 5 PPG sensors with a custom strain-gauge architecture to deliver autonomic nervous system characterisation and HRV-based coaching.

Nemow extends these principles with proprietary multi-wavelength, multi-detector PPG specifically optimised for phase-locked female physiology — meaning the sensor configuration and machine learning models are trained specifically on the hormonal variability patterns of the menstrual cycle.
