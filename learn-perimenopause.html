Perimenopause: The Signs and the Science
verifiedReviewed by Dr. Lena Okoro, OB-GYN · 5 min read

Perimenopause — the transition into menopause — typically begins in your 40s and can last 4–8 years. It is not a disease; it is a natural physiological transition. But understanding what is happening hormonally helps you navigate the changes with less confusion and more confidence.

🔄 What Changes

Cycles become irregular — shorter or longer, sometimes with skipped months. Estrogen becomes erratic — surging some cycles, dropping rapidly in others. Perimenopausal women can have high estrogen (heavy, clotty periods, breast tenderness) one month and low estrogen (hot flushes, dryness) the next. Sleep quality often declines — night sweats and changing hormone levels disrupt deep sleep.

🧪 Hormonal Markers

FSH begins rising (typically > 15–25 mIU/mL in the early follicular phase). AMH declines to low or undetectable levels. Estradiol fluctuates wildly — higher than ever at some cycles, then very low. These swings are normal, not a sign that anything is wrong.

💛 What Helps

Strength training — builds muscle and bone density, which decline as estrogen falls. Sleep hygiene — cooling the bedroom, avoiding alcohol before bed. Stress management — cortisol worsens perimenopausal symptoms. Nutrition — protein ≥ 1.6 g/kg, calcium-rich foods, and consistent mealtimes for metabolic stability. HRT/MHT — hormone therapy can be transformative for many women; discuss with your clinician within 10 years of menopause.
