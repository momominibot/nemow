\ud83d\udd25 Moxibustion

Moderate evidence for PCOS / fertility adjuncts (heterogeneous trials)

Burning dried mugwort (moxa) near acupuncture points to warm channels and move qi. Best studied for PCOS and infertility as an adjunct therapy.

\ud83e\uddca Warm Compress

Moderate evidence for dysmenorrhea relief

A simple, safe alternative to moxa. Use a warm (not hot) water bottle or microwaveable pack over the lower abdomen.

1
Fill a hot water bottle with warm water (around 40\u201345\u00b0C / 104\u2013113\u00b0F).
2
Place a thin cloth between the bottle and skin.
3
Rest for 15\u201320 minutes over the lower abdomen or lower back.
4
Remove if skin becomes red, numb, or uncomfortable.
Avoid during pregnancy over lower abdomen. Use cautiously with vascular disease or reduced sensation.

\ud83c\udf3f Herbal Poultice

Limited clinical evidence

A cloth pack filled with warm herbs applied to the abdomen. Ginger and mugwort are common warming choices in TCM.

1
Steep 30 g dried mugwort or ginger in hot water for 10 minutes.
2
Strain and wrap herbs in a clean cotton cloth.
3
Apply warm (test on wrist) over lower abdomen for 15 minutes.
4
Discard herbs after one use.
Pregnancy contraindicated. Stop if irritation occurs.

\ud83e\udeb6 Castor Oil Pack

Very low direct evidence

Western naturopathic practice, not classical TCM. Applied to lower abdomen with a warm cloth. Ricinoleic acid may have anti-inflammatory effects when absorbed through skin.

1
Apply organic castor oil to a cotton flannel cloth.
2
Place over lower abdomen; cover with plastic wrap and a warm towel.
3
Rest 20\u201345 minutes. Relax and breathe slowly.
4
Clean skin with baking soda solution; store cloth in a sealed bag.
Pregnancy contraindicated. Avoid over open skin. May stain fabrics. Allergy to castor beans is rare but possible.
Stop and seek help if:
  • cancel Redness or blistering lasts >30 min
  • cancel Dizziness, nausea, or headache from smoke/heat
  • cancel Unusual pain or burning sensation
